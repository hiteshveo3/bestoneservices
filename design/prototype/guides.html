Cleaning guides

Useful preparation, without the confusing extras.

Three practical guides to help you organise the property, prepare for handover and share clearer information when requesting an end of tenancy cleaning quote.

Guide 01

End of tenancy cleaning checklist

Start by removing personal belongings and rubbish. A clear property makes it easier to see the condition of surfaces and identify areas that need additional attention.

Before the cleaning date

Confirm the handover or inspection deadline.
Remove personal items and dispose of rubbish.
Empty cupboards if internal cleaning is required.
Share parking, keys and access instructions.
Mention oven, carpet or upholstery requirements.
Photograph existing damage separately.

The agreed cleaning scope should be confirmed before work is arranged. A checklist is preparation guidance, not a guarantee against deposit deductions.

Useful timing

Where possible, plan cleaning after the property is cleared and before the final inspection. Share any fixed key return deadline in the first enquiry.

Guide 02

Preparing a property for handover

A smoother handover depends on more than cleaning. Access, inventory timing, utilities, keys and communication with the landlord or agent can all affect the final day.

A clear sequence

  1. Check the tenancy or management documents for the expected handover condition.
  2. Confirm when belongings and furniture will leave the property.
  3. Arrange the cleaning date around access and the inspection deadline.
  4. Share the property type, bedroom count, postcode and any difficult-access details.
  5. Confirm the agreed cleaning scope and keep the final response available.

General guidance cannot replace the terms of a tenancy agreement, inventory report or professional assessment of the property.

Keys and entry

Explain whether someone will be present, keys need collection, building reception controls entry or the property has a fixed access window.

Guide 03

Furnished or unfurnished cleaning

Furniture changes how much of the room is accessible and which surfaces may need attention. Describe the property accurately so the quote can be reviewed against the real situation.

Details worth sharing

  • Whether the property is fully furnished, partly furnished or empty.
  • Whether large items will remain in bedrooms or living spaces.
  • Whether storage areas need internal cleaning and will be empty.
  • Whether upholstery or carpet cleaning is required and available.
  • Whether furniture restricts access to walls, floors or windows.

Photos can be useful when a property has unusual access, heavy furniture or a condition that is difficult to describe in a short message.

Make the quote clearer

A short note such as “partly furnished, sofa and two beds remain” is often more useful than simply selecting “furnished”.
