Lab 01 · Name and philosophy

Three names for the Bestone system

Sakina and Itqan took Arabic words because they serve UAE brands. Bestone is London-wide and English only, so its system is named from British trade and from London itself. Each concept below has a meaning, a reason, a philosophy and a first look at how it would appear on screen.

What I heard

  • Mr Reliable

    A practical local foreman with a professional firm's paperwork.

  • Landlords first

    One property or many, plus the agents who manage them.

  • Price before

    No one else in London pest control shows a price upfront.

  • On record

    Invoice, before and after photos, treatment report for every job.

  • Own staff

    Uniformed team, branded vans, no franchise in between.

A

Touchstone

Recommended

Goldsmiths tested gold by rubbing it on a dark stone and reading the streak it left. That stone was the touchstone. In everyday English it now means the standard everything else is judged by.

Why it fits Bestone

  • Bestone ends in stone. The one-word name the client chose becomes the root of the system instead of something to work around.
  • Landlords and agents buy proof. The price comes before the visit and the report comes after it.
  • Rentokil gives no price until a surveyor visits, and JG wins on review volume. Bestone wins on proof anyone can check.
Say it plainly. Prove it on the page.
  • Every claim carries its proof: a figure, a registration number or a report.
  • The price appears before anyone has to ask for it.
  • Every job leaves a record of who, what, where and when.

It should feel

  • Certain
  • Calm
  • Respected
Colour
Portland stone page (the pale limestone St Paul's is built from), charcoal text from the uniform, lime as the streak that marks what passed.
Type
An engineered grotesk (Archivo) for headings, with tabular figures for every price, date and number.
Motif
Marks. A traditional British hallmark carries four punches: maker, standard, assay office and date. A Bestone job record carries four too: who did it, the standard agreed, where and when. Four marks, like the four petals in the logo.
Shape
Small punch shapes for marks only. Everything else is quiet, flat surface.

Watch out: used too often, marks turn into badges. They stay small, and each one carries a real fact.

B

Sorted

Everyday British for "dealt with, nothing left to worry about". It is the word a customer uses when telling a friend how the job went.

Why it fits Bestone

  • It is Mr Reliable's own voice: plain, local and calm.
  • It answers the tenant who finds mice at 11pm before any feature does.
  • Every service ends the same way, whatever the trade.
One clear next step, always.
  • Every screen answers "what happens next?"
  • Every job shows where it stands, from booked to report sent.
  • Short words, big targets, no dead ends.

It should feel

  • Relieved
  • Looked after
  • Moving
Colour
White page. Lime is kept for what is done and what is happening now.
Type
A warm, heavy grotesk (Bricolage Grotesque, already on the site) at generous sizes.
Motif
The job ticket. Status fills segment by segment: booked, on the way, done, report sent.
Shape
Generous corners and large touch targets.

Watch out: slang reads casual. It carries less of the paperwork story landlords and agents need, and it can drift toward a generic app look.

C

Threshold

The doorstep, where every job starts and ends. Keys change hands, the technician introduces themselves and the work is signed off.

Why it fits Bestone

  • Bestone's staff walk into people's homes. Trust is decided at the door.
  • A tenancy check-out is a handover at the threshold.
  • London's Victorian terraces have tiled front paths, a local ornament no competitor uses.
Everything clear before the door opens.
  • Show who is coming: name, photo and ID.
  • Show what it costs and what will happen.
  • Close with a clean handover: report, invoice, sign-off.

It should feel

  • Safe
  • Respected
  • Welcome
Colour
Warm white page, lime with a soft tile grey.
Type
A friendly humanist sans (Albert Sans).
Motif
Front-path tiles: Victorian geometric patterns redrawn from the logo's four-petal mark, laid with gaps instead of lines. Door-number plaques for step numbers.
Shape
Tile geometry: squares, diamonds and four-lobed flowers.

Watch out: pattern fields sit close to Itqan's khatam ornament, and this is the most decorative of the three.

My recommendation

Touchstone

It is the only name that grows out of "Bestone" itself. It matches what landlords and agents actually buy: the price before, the proof after. Its motif is precise rather than decorative, which keeps it clearly apart from Itqan, and it scales to the unglamorous parts: invoices, treatment reports, the admin queue and the vans.

How to reply

Pick A, B or C, or a mix such as "A with B's voice". If a word or idea in any of them feels wrong, say so. That is as useful as a pick.

The other two still contribute

  • From Sorted"Leave it with us" becomes the voice line for urgent pest pages, and the status ticket becomes the booking tracker.
  • From ThresholdThe "who's coming" card (name, photo, ID) becomes a core part of every confirmation.

House rules

  • No shadows
  • Space separates, not lines
  • No dark sections
  • Lime is never text
  • Photos as tall as their copy
  • No empty grid cells
  • Titles of two or three words
  • Nothing looks browser-drawn
  • Calm motion
  • Proof points are facts only
  • New for Bestone: pests are never pictured. Show the fix.
